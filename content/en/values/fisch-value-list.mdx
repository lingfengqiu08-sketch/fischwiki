## How to Read Fisch Trading Values

An item may show as many as three prices. These figures come from different valuation systems, so combining them without understanding their purpose can lead to major mistakes.

| Value Type | What It Represents | Best Use | Important Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| TrueVal | Community-maintained value in S$ | Establishing a standard asking price | May lag behind sudden market changes |
| Trade Hub | Median S$ price from completed trades | Checking what traders have recently paid | Thin sales volume can distort the median |
| Proto | Older relative-value scale | Comparing collectible rarity and historical standing | Cannot be directly compared with S$ |
| Demand | How strongly players currently want the item | Predicting how quickly an item may trade | Popularity can change after events or updates |
| Stability | Whether prices are holding or fluctuating | Estimating short-term risk | A “stable” label does not guarantee the next offer |

Never compare a Proto figure directly with an S$ figure. For example, Evangeline’s Proto value of 2,250 does not mean it is worth S$2,250. Its listed TrueVal and Trade Hub value are both approximately S$10.8 million.

Abbreviations in the Fisch value list include:

- **K:** Thousand, so S$700K means S$700,000.
- **M:** Million, so S$4.5M means S$4,500,000.
- **N/A:** Not enough reliable information exists for that price type.
- **Stable:** Community reports suggest that the item’s value is holding.
- **Hoarded:** Owners may be withholding supply, potentially creating an inflated or difficult market.
- **Manipulated:** The displayed price may be influenced by unusual trading activity or coordinated speculation.

Demand matters alongside price. A high-demand item close to its list value can be easier to trade than an obscure collectible with a higher theoretical valuation.
